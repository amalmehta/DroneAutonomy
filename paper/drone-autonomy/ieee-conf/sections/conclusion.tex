\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

We combined seven meta-learning algorithms with a classical quadrotor stack at three insertion points and found that where learning enters matters more than which algorithm is used. A robust learned residual captures most of the tracking benefit without meta-learning. Meta-learning pays off when it adapts the classical controller's gains. In navigation, gradient-based adaptation at meta-trained step sizes made learned planners less safe, while a classical planner on an online map remained the most reliable. Every method failed beyond the training distribution, with learned residuals crashing more than the classical controller. These results argue for keeping classical components in the loop, adapting their parameters with conservative updates, and testing adaptation out of distribution before trusting it on a real vehicle. Flight tests on the recommended platform are the next step.
