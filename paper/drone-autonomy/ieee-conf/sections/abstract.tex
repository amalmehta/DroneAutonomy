\begin{abstract}
A quadrotor deployed outside the lab meets conditions it was not tuned for: extra payload, a worn motor, wind and command latency. Meta-reinforcement learning promises adaptation from a few flights, while classical stacks built on geometric control, adaptive control, occupancy mapping and A* planning are dependable but fixed. We ask where meta-learning helps when it is combined with such a stack. In a simulator parameterised after a Holybro X500 V2, we insert learning at three points (a residual on a geometric controller, the controller's gains, and the local planner of a depth-camera, mapping and A* navigation stack) and compare seven algorithms (MAML, FOMAML, ANIL, Meta-SGD, Reptile, PEARL and RL$^2$) with nominal, L1-adaptive, domain-randomised and fine-tuning baselines on held-out and out-of-distribution dynamics. A learned residual roughly halves tracking error relative to tuned L1 adaptation (\valResDrPost\ vs \valLoneTunedTest\,cm), and meta-learning adds little to it. Meta-learning pays off when it adapts the classical gains: Meta-SGD lowers error from \valGainMetasgdPre\ to \valGainMetasgdPost\,cm, \valGainPairedDiff\,cm better than non-meta tuning on paired tasks. In navigation, gradient-based adaptation at the meta-trained step size degrades learned planners, and across \valNavFullFlights\ flights in unseen rooms the classical local planner is the most reliable (\valNavClassicalFullPost\% success, \valNavClassicalFullColl\% collisions). Outside the training distribution every method fails, and learned residuals crash more often than the classical controller. We release the suite and recommend a platform and staged plan for real-world tests.
\end{abstract}
