\textbf{A learned residual beats classical adaptation.} On held-out tasks the nominal geometric controller tracks with \valResClassicalPost\,cm RMSE and L1 augmentation with \valResLonePost\,cm (Table~\ref{tab:tracking}). A DR residual trained over all tasks reaches \valResDrPost\,cm without any adaptation, \valResRatioClassical$\times$ and \valResRatioLone$\times$ lower respectively.

\textbf{Gradient-based meta-learning adds little to the residual.} MAML, FOMAML, ANIL and Meta-SGD start slightly worse than DR (\valResMamlPre, \valResFomamlPre, \valResAnilPre\ and \valResMetasgdPre\,cm) and improve with 30 adaptation episodes to \valResMamlPost, \valResFomamlPost, \valResAnilPost\ and \valResMetasgdPost\,cm, within the interval of DR's \valResDrPost\,cm (Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}a). Fine-tuning DR with PPO makes it slightly worse (\valResDrftPost\,cm), and Reptile trails at \valResReptilePost\,cm. With a broad task distribution and a strong classical controller underneath, a single robust residual already captures most of the benefit in our setup.

\textbf{Meta-learning helps most when it tunes gains.} When the policy chooses the controller's seven gains (Combination 2), adaptation matters more: Meta-SGD improves from \valGainMetasgdPre\ to \valGainMetasgdPost\,cm, and MAML and FOMAML reach \valGainMamlPost\ and \valGainFomamlPost\,cm. DR-tuned gains stay at \valGainDrPost\,cm, or \valGainDrftPost\,cm with fine-tuning (Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}b). This result is preliminary: with two seeds, Meta-SGD's interval ($\pm$\valGainMetasgdCi\,cm) only just separates from the fine-tuned DR gains ($\pm$\valGainDrftCi\,cm). Tuned gains remain well above the learned residual (\valResDrPost\,cm), but a gain vector is a far smaller and more interpretable thing to adapt on a real vehicle.

\textbf{Context-based methods adapt from fewer episodes.} PEARL improves from \valResPearlPre\ to \valResPearlPost\,cm with three episodes of context, against the 30 episodes the gradient methods use, though it does not reach their final error. RL$^2$ improves from \valResRltwoPre\ to \valResRltwoPost\,cm and is the weakest learned residual.

\textbf{Adaptation hurts learned navigation planners, except RL$^2$.} With the privileged planner, every warm-started gradient method loses success as it adapts: MAML from \valNavMamlPre\% to \valNavMamlPost\%, FOMAML from \valNavFomamlPre\% to \valNavFomamlPost\%, ANIL from \valNavAnilPre\% to \valNavAnilPost\%, Meta-SGD from \valNavMetasgdPre\% to \valNavMetasgdPost\% and Reptile from \valNavReptilePre\% to \valNavReptilePost\% (Table~\ref{tab:nav}, Fig.~\ref{fig:curves}c). DR with fine-tuning also drops, from \valNavDrftPre\% to \valNavDrftPost\%. Collision rates rise as success falls, so the updates trade safety away. RL$^2$ is the only method that improves with experience, from \valNavRltwoPre\% to \valNavRltwoPost\%, and PEARL stays flat (\valNavPearlPre\% to \valNavPearlPost\%). We read this as noisy policy gradients from 10 sparse-reward episodes per task overwriting a competent planner, while RL$^2$ adapts without gradient steps. This is one seed, so the effect needs confirming.

\textbf{On the full stack, the classical planner is most reliable.} With a map built in flight and A* replanning, the classical local planner reaches the goal in \valNavClassicalFullPost\% of the 12 unseen rooms with \valNavClassicalFullColl\% collisions. The learned planners reach between \valNavFullLearnedLo\% and \valNavFullLearnedHi\% with \valNavFullCollLo--\valNavFullCollHi\% collisions. With 12 rooms one room moves a rate by 8 points, so we treat these numbers as indicative: the classical planner was never worse, and it never collided. Fig.~\ref{fig:flight} shows a recorded classical flight.

\textbf{The map and planner matter.} The end-to-end DR planner, which sees only the goal direction, succeeds in \valNavEtoePre\% of the 24 held-out rooms, against \valNavDrPre\% for the same learner given the carrot, and in \valNavEtoeFullPost\% against \valNavDrFullPost\% on the full stack.

\textbf{Outside the training distribution every method fails.} On the OOD tasks, which combine heavier payload, weaker motors, stronger wind and longer latency, no tracking method gets below \valOodRmseMin\,cm RMSE and no navigation method exceeds \valNavOodMax\% success. Learned residuals also crash more often (\valResOodCrashLearnedLo--\valResOodCrashLearnedHi\%) than the nominal controller (\valResClassicalOodCrash\%). Adaptation does not rescue them: three stages change OOD error by at most \valOodAdaptMaxDelta\,cm. Our OOD set is harsh, so it measures how methods fail more than how they adapt, but the ordering is a caution for deployment: on the edge of the envelope, the plain classical controller degraded most gracefully.
